About

I’m a compositor who likes solving the workflow around the shot.

My background is in Nuke compositing, but I keep moving toward the technical side of production: artist support, Nuke/Python tools, workflow troubleshooting, and practical pipeline habits.

I’m most interested in the space between artists, tools, and production needs. I like understanding what an artist is trying to do, separating the symptom from the real problem, and finding a fix that is clear, safe, and easy to explain.

Short summary

I have production and freelance compositing experience in Nuke, including cleanup, roto, tracking, element integration, ACES/EXR workflows, and review-driven delivery. Alongside that, I build public Nuke/Python tools focused on safer, more repeatable artist workflows.

My current direction is toward Assistant Technical Director, Pipeline Support, Nuke Tools, Production Technology, and related studio technical-support roles where compositing knowledge and practical troubleshooting both matter.

Artist support

How I support artists

I approach technical problems from the artist side first: understand the goal, separate symptoms from root causes, inspect the setup, apply the smallest safe fix, and explain what changed so the workflow is easier to repeat.

Nuke script troubleshooting

I help isolate whether a Nuke problem is coming from the script, a node, a gizmo, a bounding box, heavy processing, or a workflow assumption.

  • Crashing or unstable .nk files
  • Slow scripts, heavy nodes, and large bounding boxes
  • Broken or outdated gizmos
  • Safe fixes that preserve the artist’s work

Maya-to-Nuke handoffs

I look for the workflow gaps that create downstream confusion between lighting/rendering and compositing.

  • Required AOVs and frame ranges
  • ACES / color-management consistency
  • Naming, versioning, and render locations
  • Project-relative paths for assets and textures

Render / environment issues

I try to determine whether the real problem is the scene, the render settings, missing resources, or a mismatch between local and farm environments.

  • Arnold render-cost diagnosis and optimization
  • Redshift render farm path/version mismatches
  • Texture, cache, and project-structure issues
  • Lower-cost fixes that preserve the intended look

Workflow systems

When a problem repeats, I try to turn it into a clearer process, checklist, shared setup, or artist-facing tool.

  • Folder structures, naming, and versioning rules
  • Shared and personal Nuke tool libraries
  • Safe Nuke tools with confirmations and clear boundaries
  • Documentation for recurring artist problems

My usual support process

  1. Understand what the artist is trying to achieve.
  2. Separate the visible symptom from the likely root cause.
  3. Check versions, paths, settings, dependencies, and recent changes.
  4. Apply the smallest safe fix instead of changing the whole setup.
  5. Explain what changed and document recurring issues when useful.
